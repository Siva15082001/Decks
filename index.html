01 / Context

02 / Problem

The Technology Is Ready. Activation and Positioning Are Not.

Caper Cart adoption is not growing at the pace the business needs. Deployment is still early compared with Instacart's overall partner base, and store-level consistency varies. Hardware maturity matters, but the biggest near-term gap is activation and repeat behavior.


Caper Cart has often been framed as skip the checkout line. For many shoppers, checkout speed is useful but not the core reason to return. The stronger repeat driver is in-trip value: running total visibility and relevant savings in the moment.


The product was built on strong technology, but the value story needs to lead with financial control and confidence, not only convenience.

03 / Insight

The AI Exists. The In-Store Habit Loop Needs Better Design.

A large share of grocery trips are quick-fill missions, so not every trip is a Caper Cart trip. The highest-value segment is planned, basket-building, budget-conscious shoppers who already intend to use a cart.


That shopper does not need a faster checkout experience. They need a smarter one. They want to see their running total before they reach the register. They want a relevant deal surfaced when they walk past the dairy aisle, not after the moment has passed.


The infrastructure to deliver this already exists. Caper Cart can combine computer vision, cart context, and loyalty-linked signals to surface relevant savings in real time. The AI capability is meaningful; the onboarding and value communication now need to match it.

04 / Solution

Position Caper Cart as a Smart Savings Copilot, with Faster Checkout as a Bonus

The running total becomes the hero feature, not a utility tucked in the corner. Personalized coupons surface proactively based on what is already in the cart and where the shopper is standing in the store. Onboarding leads with "see exactly what you are spending and save as you go" instead of "skip the line."

This is not a rebuild. It is a targeted repositioning validated through one focused experiment: change the first-interaction screen and onboarding prompt, run it across three retail partners, and measure whether savings-framed entry improves cart attach rate and session engagement.

05 / 60-Day Acceleration Plan

What I Would Do in the First 60 Days

1
Orient and Ship
Days 1 – 20

  • Week 1: Embed with Connected Stores, Data Science, and Retail Ops. Lock baseline definitions for activation, repeat, and savings interaction.

  • Week 2: Shadow two retailer deployments. Run rapid in-store intercepts and identify first-use friction moments.

  • Week 3: Deliver friction audit to Design/Eng and finalize Experiment A: checkout-speed-first vs savings-first onboarding.

  • By Day 20: Experiment A is live at one partner with store-level associate script + signage support.

2
Expand and Validate
Days 21 – 40

  • Scale winner from Experiment A to three stores/partner cohorts. Pull first read by day 30.

  • Run targeted interviews focused on budget-conscious repeat users vs first-time drop-offs.

  • Launch Experiment B: proactive savings nudges by aisle + cart context, with relevance thresholds and frequency caps.

  • Present midpoint recommendation to leadership by day 40 with go/no-go scale criteria.

3
Ship and Set Direction
Days 41 – 60

  • Read out combined results. Instrument permanent events around savings behavior and repeat activation.

  • Operationalize the winning messaging and onboarding pattern with an execution playbook for Store Ops + Partner teams.

  • By day 60, deliver a prioritized roadmap with explicit impact assumptions, resource asks, and scale sequencing by partner readiness.
